ETF / Basket Arbitrage — the plain-English tour

What a basket is, why the trade is mechanical, and how to find the weights.

The story

Imagine a vending machine. The vending machine sells "Baskets" — gift baskets with 4 chocolates, 6 strawberries, and 1 rose. The machine has a price tag: $50 per basket. You can also buy the individual items from the same store: chocolate $2, strawberry $1, rose $20.

What's the basket worth on its own? 4 × $2 + 6 × $1 + 1 × $20 = $8 + $6 + $20 = $34. But the vending machine sells it for $50. So the basket is "expensive" — overpriced relative to its parts.

The trade: sell a basket for $50, and buy the parts for $34. You pocket the $16 difference. Once the parts are in your hand and the basket is sold, the trade is done. Risk-free.

This is basket arbitrage. It's mechanical. It works whenever the basket price diverges from the sum of its parts by more than the round-trip cost (fees, slippage, holding risk).

What is a basket?

A basket is a synthetic instrument defined as a fixed combination of other real instruments. In Prosperity:

GIFT_BASKET = 4 × CHOCOLATE + 6 × STRAWBERRIES + 1 × ROSES

The "4", "6", "1" are the weights. They are given in the round spec — read the spec carefully. Sometimes they're explicit (GIFT_BASKET). Sometimes they're implicit (PEBBLE: "5 assets sum to 50,000" — you have to figure out the weights from the data).

The basket itself trades on the order book, just like any other instrument. You can buy and sell it. Its price is determined by market dynamics — the same supply and demand that affects any tradeable thing.

What is NAV?

NAV = Net Asset Value. For a basket, it's the sum of (weight × price) of all constituents at the current moment:

NAV = 4 × chocolate_price + 6 × strawberry_price + 1 × rose_price

Every tick, the basket's market price is whatever the latest trade was. The NAV is whatever the current prices of the constituents are. They can disagree.

The trade

Three states:

  1. basket_price > NAV + threshold: basket is overpriced. Sell basket, buy constituents. Pocket the difference when the spread closes.
  2. basket_price < NAV − threshold: basket is underpriced. Buy basket, sell constituents.
  3. |basket_price − NAV| < threshold: no edge. Don't trade.

The threshold covers fees + slippage + holding cost. Tune it from historical data.

Why does this work?

Real markets enforce this arbitrage through "authorized participants" who can create and redeem ETF shares. If the ETF price goes too high, APs sell the ETF and buy the constituents, redeeming for more shares. If the ETF price goes too low, APs create new shares and buy the constituents. The arbitrage keeps the price near NAV.

In Prosperity, there's no automatic AP. But the team that solves the structural arb still wins — other teams are dumb and the market doesn't auto-correct. The trade captures the spread when the market is dumb.

Finding the weights

Two ways:

  1. Read the spec. GIFT_BASKET's spec says "4 chocolates, 6 strawberries, 1 rose." Hardcode 4, 6, 1.
  2. Recover from data. PEBBLE says "5 assets sum to 50,000." You don't know the weights. Solve them from the data using OLS: regress basket price on the leg prices. The coefficients are the weights.

Method 2 is more general. The spec sometimes lies, sometimes uses different units (e.g., the basket might be in cents and the legs in dollars). Always cross-check the spec weights against an OLS fit on past data.

The two flavors of basket arb

  1. Spread arb (GIFT_BASKET style): trade the basket against the legs. Capture the spread when it diverges. Has directional risk (the basket and legs can both move against you before the spread closes). Manage inventory.
  2. Inner rebalancing (PEBBLE style): when 5 assets are constrained to sum to 50,000, the constraint enforces an arbitrage. If asset A is rich and the others are cheap, sell A and buy the others. The sum stays 50,000. The trade is self-financing and zero-risk.

Spread arb is the simpler one and is most rounds. Inner rebalancing is the dream when it applies — PnL is mathematically guaranteed.

Why this round type is so high-PnL

Other teams often do basket arb wrong:

  • They get the weights wrong (use the spec literally without cross-checking).
  • They forget to handle illiquid legs (you can't always fill the leg side).
  • They don't size correctly (the legs might be 4× and 6× — your position in the basket is small but your position in the legs is large).
  • They trade the spread without an inventory cap (one leg moves against you, the spread blows out, you blow up).

Get these right and the edge is consistent. P3 Timo used basic spread arb on GIFT_BASKET and won 2nd place globally.
