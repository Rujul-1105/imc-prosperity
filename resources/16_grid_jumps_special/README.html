What you'll know after this module: Why a GBM projected onto a coarse price grid creates tradeable jumps. How to estimate the "true" (unrounded) price from a sequence of grid prices. How to predict the next grid jump. How to size the trade. How to backtest on synthetic and past data.

Why this module is so important

From Leo Hawking's P4 retrospective: the 96th-ranked team would have moved to top 15 by trading ±100 grid jumps on the coarse-grid GBM round. The edge is so specific to the round type that most teams miss it entirely. If you build this module, you capture the alpha that other teams leave on the table.

What is a grid jump?

A geometric Brownian motion (GBM) projected onto a coarse price grid. The true price follows `dS = σ S dW`. The exchange rounds prices to a grid (e.g., 1.0 increments). The displayed price is `round(S / grid) * grid`. When the true price moves more than the grid size between ticks, the displayed price "jumps" by the grid size.

These jumps are predictable from a smoothed estimate of the true price. That's the edge.
