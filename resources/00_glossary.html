Glossary

Every term used in the curriculum, alphabetized. Each definition is plain English — no jargon, no formulas. Cross-links point to where the term is taught.

A

Adverse selection

The risk that the person willing to trade with you knows something you don't. If you keep quoting a buy price of $100 and a smarter trader keeps selling to you, they only do it when they think the true price is below $100. You get picked off. Market-makers care a lot about this. → module 02

Ask (offer)

The lowest price at which someone is willing to sell the asset. The "sell" side of the order book. See bid-ask spread.

Avellaneda–Stoikov

A famous formula (Avellaneda & Stoikov, 2008) for what prices a market-maker should quote. It balances making money on the spread against the risk of holding inventory. → module 03

B

Basket

A fake "asset" defined as a fixed combination of other real assets. E.g., GIFT_BASKET = 4 chocolates + 6 strawberries + 1 rose. If you can buy the basket and sell the pieces (or vice versa) for more than the basket price, you have a risk-free trade. → module 06

Bid

The highest price at which someone is willing to buy the asset. The "buy" side of the order book.

Bid-ask spread

The difference between the best bid and the best ask. If bid is $99 and ask is $101, the spread is $2. The market-maker who posted those quotes makes $2 per round-trip trade (buy at $99, sell at $101). → module 02

Black–Scholes

A formula for what an option should be worth. Inputs are the underlying price, strike, time-to-expiry, risk-free rate, and volatility. Outputs are call/put prices plus four "Greeks" (sensitivities). → module 07

Book depth

How many buy/sell orders are stacked at each price level. Deep book = lots of liquidity = your trade barely moves the price. Thin book = small trade can move price a lot.

C

Call option

A contract that gives the holder the right but not obligation to buy the underlying asset at a fixed "strike" price on or before expiry. Worth more when the underlying price is above the strike. → module 07

Cointegration

Two prices that wander around randomly individually but their difference stays roughly constant. If chocolate and cocoa-prices are cointegrated, you can bet on their gap closing. → module 05

Cross-exchange arb

Same product, two exchanges, different prices. Buy low on one, sell high on the other, lock in the spread. Prosperity rounds that have two exchanges (ORCHIDS, MACARONS) reward this. → module 08

D

Delta (Greek)

How much an option's price moves when the underlying moves by $1. Delta of 0.5 means option goes up $0.50 for each $1 underlying rally. You "delta-hedge" by holding underlying so total delta = 0. → module 07

Drawdown

The peak-to-trough decline of your PnL. If your PnL was +500 and is now +120, you have a 380 drawdown. Survivorship cares a lot about how deep drawdowns get.

E

EMA (exponential moving average)

A smoothed average of recent prices where recent prices count more. EMA(20) of price is a rolling mean that forgets the distant past fast. Used as a "fair value" estimate.

ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)

A fund that holds a basket of assets and trades like a stock. The "creation" process lets authorized participants swap shares for the underlying — that's what keeps ETF price and NAV aligned. → module 06

Exchange score

See NAV. In Prosperity, baskets behave like ETFs.

F

Fair value

The price you think the asset should be at, given everything you know. Trade when the market price is far from fair value. → module 11

FILL OR KILL

An order that must execute immediately in full or be cancelled. The opposite of a limit order that rests on the book.

G

Gamma (Greek)

How fast delta changes. High gamma means small underlying moves cause big delta swings, so delta-hedging gets expensive. → module 07

GARCH

A model that estimates today's volatility from recent squared returns. Volatility clusters (calm then stormy then calm) and GARCH captures that pattern. → module 10

Greeks

Delta, gamma, vega, theta — the four main sensitivities of an option's price to its inputs. Named with Greek letters. → module 07

H

Half-life

The typical time it takes a mean-reverting process to close half its gap to equilibrium. Half-life of 5 ticks means z-score of 2 typically decays to 1 in 5 ticks. → module 04

Hedging

Taking an offsetting position to cancel out some unwanted exposure. Short the S&P to hedge a long stock portfolio. Sell calls against your long stock to hedge upside. → module 07

I

Implied volatility (IV)

The volatility number you'd have to plug into Black–Scholes to make it spit out the market price of the option. If everyone is nervous, options cost more → IV is high. → module 07

Inventory

Your current position. If you've bought 50 and sold 30, inventory is +20 (long 20). Market-makers carry inventory and get picked off when it goes against them. → module 03

Inventory skew

Adjusting your quoted prices to push your inventory back toward zero. If you're long 100, lower both your bid and your ask to encourage selling to you. → module 03

K

Kelly criterion

A formula for the optimal bet size given your edge and odds. In trading: f* = (edge / odds). Often used at fractional Kelly (½ or ¼) for safety. → module 14

L

Limit order

An order to buy/sell at a specified price or better. Sits on the book until filled or cancelled. The opposite of a market order.

Limit order book (LOB)

The list of all unfilled limit orders, sorted by price. Buy side and sell side. The whole world of market-making lives in this list. → module 02

Liquidity

How easily you can trade without moving the price. A deep book with tight spreads is liquid; a thin book with wide spreads is illiquid.

Long / short

Long = you own the asset (positive position). Short = you've sold something you don't own and must return it later (negative position).

M

Market impact

The price movement caused by your own trade. A big market order eats through the book and pushes the price. The deeper the book, the less impact per share.

Market order

An order to buy/sell right now at the best available price. Fills immediately but you pay the spread. Opposite of limit order.

Market making

Posting both buy and sell limit orders and earning the spread. The core game in Prosperity's AMETHYSTS-style rounds. → module 03

Mean reversion

The price wanders but is pulled back toward a center. Trade: bet that a big move will reverse. → module 04

Microprice

A more accurate mid than just (bid+ask)/2. Microprice weights the inside levels by volume: (bid_volume * ask + ask_volume * bid) / (bid_volume + ask_volume). If bid has more size, microprice leans up. → module 02

Mid price

(Best bid + best ask) / 2. The "center" of the spread. A first approximation of fair value.

N

NAV (Net Asset Value)

For a basket: the sum of its constituents at current market prices (using the formula). If the basket market price is below NAV, buy basket / sell constituents. → module 06

O

Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU)

The math model behind mean reversion. Continuous-time: dx = θ(μ − x)dt + σdW. Discretized: x_{t+1} = α + β·x_t + ε. The β tells you how strongly it pulls back. → module 04

P

Pairs trading

Find two prices that move together (cointegrated). When their gap is wide, bet the gap closes. → module 05

PnL (Profit and Loss)

The running total of how much money you've made. Marked-to-market: at each instant, what would you make if you flattened your book right now. The score Prosperity ranks you on.

Position

Same as inventory. The number of units you currently hold (positive = long, negative = short).

Put option

A contract that gives the holder the right but not obligation to sell the underlying at a fixed strike on or before expiry. Worth more when the underlying is below the strike. → module 07

Q

Queue priority

If 100 people are all bidding $99, who fills first when a market sell comes in? First one in line. Prosperity's exchange has no queue priority by default (pro-rata), which creates its own alpha. → module 17

Quote

A pair of orders: a bid and an ask. "What are you quoting?" = "What are your bid and ask right now?"

R

Reservation price

The price at which you'd be indifferent between trading and not trading. Set by your inventory and the volatility. Avellaneda–Stoikov centers your bid/ask around this. → module 03

Round-trip

Open a position, then close it. Profit = sell price − buy price − fees. The PnL from one complete cycle.

S

Sharpe ratio

Mean return divided by standard deviation of returns. Risk-adjusted performance. Sharpe > 1 is decent; > 2 is good; > 3 is exceptional.

Slippage

The difference between the price you expected to get and the price you actually got. Usually because the book moved while your order was in flight, or your order ate through multiple levels.

Spread

See bid-ask spread. The market-maker's gross profit per round-trip.

Stat arb

Statistical arbitrage — short-term mean-reversion / cointegration trades, often on related instruments. The Renaissance Medallion fund is the canonical example. → module 05

Stop-loss

An order to exit a position if price moves against you by a threshold. Caps your downside per trade. → module 14

T

Theta (Greek)

How much an option's value decays per day. Options lose value as expiry approaches (all else equal). → module 07

Tick

The smallest price increment. If the asset moves in steps of $0.01, that's a tick. Tick size affects strategy (wider ticks = fewer fair signals, more profit per fill).

Trend following

Bet the recent move continues. The opposite of mean reversion. → module 09

V

Vega (Greek)

How much an option's price changes when implied volatility changes by 1 percentage point. Long vega = you want vol to rise. → module 07

Volatility (σ)

How wildly the price moves. High σ = big jumps, scary. Low σ = calm. Drives option prices, market-making spreads, risk limits. → module 10

W

Wall-Mid

Timo Diehm's simpler market-making approach: post your bid at the level just inside the largest wall, your ask at the level just outside. Cheaper than Avellaneda–Stoikov, often beats it. → module 03 extract

Working order

A limit order that's resting on the book and hasn't been filled or cancelled yet. Synonym for limit order in this context.

Z

Z-score

How far the current value is from the average, measured in σ. Z = (x − mean) / std. Z = −2 means two standard deviations below the mean. Common entry threshold for mean reversion. → module 04
