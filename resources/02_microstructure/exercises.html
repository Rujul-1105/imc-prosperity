Module 02 — Exercises

Five hands-on exercises. Use code/order_book.py as your starting point.

← module README · extract

All exercises use the P3/P4 CSVs you have in ../prosperity-4/data_capsule/. If you don't have them yet, jmerle's repo has them.

Exercise 1 — Parse a CSV into an OrderBook easy · 30 min

Write code/parse_p3_csv.py that reads a round's prices CSV (e.g. ROUND_1/prices_round_1_day_-1.csv) and constructs a list of OrderBook snapshots — one per row.

Prosperity CSV columns include timestamp, product, bid_price_1, bid_volume_1, ask_price_1, ask_volume_1, bid_price_2, bid_volume_2, etc. Filter to one product. Build bids/asks lists.

Verify: print the first book; check mid, spread, microprice are all reasonable (mid ≈ visible price from the round).

Exercise 2 — Microprice vs mid on AMETHYSTS-style data 30 min

For the same parsed books, compute mid and microprice at each tick. Plot (matplotlib) the difference microprice - mid over time.

Question: is the difference usually small, or large? When is it large? Does it predict the next move?

Exercise 3 — The 1-tick-better pricing alpha 1 hour

Simulate the following strategy on P3/P4 RAINFOREST_RESIN data:

  • Every tick, post a bid at best_bid + 1 and an ask at best_ask - 1.
  • You fill whenever a market order crosses your quote.
  • Track inventory and PnL.

Because Prosperity has no queue priority, your 1-tick-better quote gets filled first. Compute the spread you capture per round-trip.

Verify: spread capture per round-trip = 2 (1 tick on each side). Inventory drift over a day should be small (symmetric strategy).

Exercise 4 — Realized spread and adverse selection 1.5 hours

For each of your fills in Exercise 3, compute the effective spread at fill and the realized spread $T = 10$ ticks later. Average over many fills.

Question: what's the average realized spread? Compare to the nominal spread (= 2). How much do you lose to adverse selection?

Hint: if you filled a buy, mark-to-market your position 10 ticks later at the new mid, add to the spread revenue, divide by 2.

Exercise 5 — Wall detection 2 hours

Extend your order book parser to find the biggest wall on each side. A wall is a single price level with volume > 3× the median top-3 volume. Plot a histogram of wall sizes and locations.

Then implement the Wall-Mid strategy from Timo's case study:

  • Place your bid at bid_wall_price + 1.
  • Place your ask at ask_wall_price - 1.

Backtest on P3/P4 RAINFOREST_RESIN and report PnL. Compare to the naïve mid strategy from Exercise 3.

Verify: Wall-Mid should beat naïve mid on average (it's the strategy that got Timo 2nd place globally). If it doesn't, debug. Most likely: you miscounted walls or your fill model is too generous.
