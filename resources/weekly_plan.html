16 weeks core + 4 advanced · both of you work most modules, some weekly for parallel pairs.

Reading time budget: ~6–8 hours reading, ~6–8 hours coding per week per person. Heavy weeks (5, 9, 11) push 12+ hours. Plan rest days — burnout is the #1 risk in 16-week prep.
Review / consolidation week Mock round or exercise-heavy Advanced (only after week 16)

How to split between two people

Most weeks are parallel — both of you read the same material and pair-program the exercises. A few weeks suggest a split:

  • Week 6 (cointegration): Person A reads Engle–Granger; Person B reads Johansen. Reconcile Thursday.
  • Week 9 (options): Person A owns Black–Scholes derivation + delta hedging; Person B owns IV smile + vol surface. Reconcile Saturday.
  • Week 11 (ML + vol): Person A owns López de Prado Ch 1–7 (XGBoost pipeline); Person B owns GARCH/EGARCH via arch package. Reconcile Sunday.
  • Week 16 (mock round): split products. Each person writes their trader's strategy solo, then merge.

What "deliverable" means

Every week ends with a commit to git. Each deliverable is one of:

  1. A finished module folder (XX_module/explain.html + source.html + extract.html + exercises.html + runnable code/).
  2. A backtest result with PnL broken down (spread / inventory / rebalance).
  3. A solved manual challenge write-up.
  4. A 1-page retrospective on the week's failures (also commit it).

You are graded by your ability to replicate a past winning pattern, not by reading speed.

You already have P3/P4 round CSVs in the sibling folder ../prosperity-4/data_capsule/ROUND_1/ etc. Use these for exercises. The P3 datasets are mirrored in jmerle's repo if you want a fresh copy.

When Prosperity drops

  1. Stop the curriculum. Don't keep grinding modules.
  2. Move into code/ (created at that point).
  3. Plug the in-house visualizer/backtester (built separately) into the loop.
  4. Per-round pattern: read the spec, identify which modules apply, pull the relevant extract.html + code/ as starting code, iterate.
  5. After each round, retro and update the relevant module's extract.html with what you learned.

Update weekly with what you actually finished vs. what you skipped. Honesty > optimism.
